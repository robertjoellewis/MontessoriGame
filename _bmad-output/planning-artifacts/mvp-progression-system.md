# MVP Progression System — Toddler Montessori Game

**Date:** 2026-02-07
**Context:** Design doc for MVP progression, lessons, and unlock pacing

## Toddler Curriculum Areas (Simplified for MVP)

### 1. Practical Life (Core Focus)
**Why it matters:** This is THE foundation for toddlers. Independence, order, concentration.

**MVP Lessons (4):**
- Handwashing
- Pouring (water, then dry materials)
- Table wiping
- Simple food prep (peeling banana, spreading)

**Materials to collect:**
- Child-sized pitcher & basin
- Sponges and cloths
- Step stool
- Aprons

---

### 2. Language (Essential)
**Why it matters:** Toddlers are in a language explosion period.

**MVP Lessons (3):**
- Object naming (vocabulary cards/objects)
- Simple books (board books, picture books)
- Songs & fingerplays

**Materials to collect:**
- Language baskets (themed object sets)
- Book shelf & books
- Song cards

---

### 3. Movement & Fine Motor
**Why it matters:** Toddlers need to MOVE and refine coordination.

**MVP Lessons (3):**
- Walking the line (balance and body awareness)
- Simple puzzles (knobbed puzzles, shape sorters)
- Threading/stacking activities

**Materials to collect:**
- Floor line (tape or painted)
- Wooden puzzles (various difficulties)
- Large beads & laces

---

### 4. Sensorial (Simplified)
**Why it matters:** Toddlers explore the world through senses.

**MVP Lessons (2):**
- Color matching (simple 3-color sort)
- Texture exploration (boards or baskets)

**Materials to collect:**
- Color tablets or colored objects
- Texture boards/fabric samples

---

### 5. Care of Environment (Integrated with Practical Life)
**Why it matters:** Toddlers love helping and need ownership.

**MVP Lessons (2):**
- Sweeping
- Plant watering

**Materials to collect:**
- Child-sized broom & dustpan
- Small watering can
- Plants

## Guide Progression Tiers

### Tier 1: Observer (Week 1-2, ~3-4 in-game days)
**You can:**
- Watch children
- Read their observation notes (hovering shows "interested in X", "frustrated with Y")
- Shadow the lead guide
- Help with diaper changes, meals (non-lesson tasks)

**You CANNOT:**
- Present lessons
- Rearrange materials
- Interact deeply with children (just basic care)

**Goal:** Learn the children's names, temperaments, and daily rhythm.

**Advancement trigger:**
- Complete "observation journal" entries for all 12 children
- Spend X minutes observing work cycle
- Pass a "readiness conversation" with lead guide (cutscene)

---

### Tier 2: Assistant (Week 3-5, ~4-6 in-game days)
**You can:**
- Present 2 Practical Life lessons: Handwashing, Pouring
- Set out materials for these lessons
- Respond to children's requests ("Can you show me?")

**You CANNOT:**
- Present other lesson types
- Change room layout
- Handle challenging behaviors alone

**Goal:** Build confidence with the simplest, most concrete lessons.

**Advancement trigger:**
- Successfully present each unlocked lesson at least twice
- Have 3 children independently repeat a lesson you taught
- Build trust meter with lead guide to 50%

---

### Tier 3: Guide-in-Training (Week 6-10, ~8-10 in-game days)
**You can:**
- Present all Practical Life and Care of Environment lessons
- Present Language lessons (naming, books, songs)
- Present Movement lessons (walking line, puzzles)
- Suggest small environment changes (move a shelf, rotate materials)

**You CANNOT:**
- Change major room layout
- Handle parent meetings alone
- Teach Sensorial (still learning to observe precisely)

**Goal:** Become competent across most of the daily routine.

**Advancement trigger:**
- Present 10+ successful lessons (across different areas)
- Resolve a "child struggling" scenario using observation (e.g., notice a child needs smaller pitcher, swap it out → success)
- Build trust meter with lead guide to 80%
- Complete a "professional development" event (attend workshop or read Montessori book)

---

### Tier 4: Lead Guide (Week 11+, endgame)
**You can:**
- Present ALL lessons (including Sensorial)
- Redesign room layout
- Hold parent-teacher meetings
- Mentor new assistants (if that becomes a feature)
- **Fix non-Montessori practices** (see mechanic below)

**Goal:** Full autonomy and mastery.

**Advancement trigger:**
- Complete all lesson types at least once
- All 12 children show growth in at least one area
- Trust meter at 100%
- Final "certification" cutscene with mentor

## Pacing Summary

**Approximate MVP playthrough:**
- **Total in-game time:** ~15-20 days (each day = 14 real-time minutes)
- **Total real-world time:** 3.5 - 4.5 hours of focused play
- **Feel:** Gentle but steady progression
- **Replayability:** Different children each playthrough, different material gacha pulls, different "problems" to solve

**Balancing notes:**
- Don't gate progress purely on time — let skilled players advance faster by completing objectives early
- Trust meter grows through: successful lessons, positive child outcomes, and responding well to scenarios
- Each tier should feel meaningfully different in what you can do

## Non-Montessori Practices Mechanic

**Concept:** When you start, the classroom has some things that aren't quite right by Montessori standards. As an Observer/Assistant, you notice them but can't change them (you're too junior). As you gain authority, you can fix them.

### Examples of "Wrong" Practices (MVP - 5 total)

1. **Plastic cups instead of glass/ceramic**
   - Notice at: Observer
   - Fix at: Guide-in-Training (suggest to lead guide)
   - Effect: Children are more careful, spills decrease, concentration improves

2. **Adult-height sink (no step stool)**
   - Notice at: Observer
   - Fix at: Assistant (you can just... add a step stool)
   - Effect: Children wash hands independently, happier

3. **Toys with batteries/lights/sounds on shelf**
   - Notice at: Assistant
   - Fix at: Guide-in-Training (have conversation with lead guide about passive vs. active learning)
   - Effect: Children engage more deeply with simpler materials

4. **Messy, unlabeled shelves**
   - Notice at: Assistant
   - Fix at: Guide-in-Training (organize and label)
   - Effect: Children choose work more independently, return materials correctly

5. **Time-pressure / rushing transitions**
   - Notice at: Guide-in-Training
   - Fix at: Lead Guide (redesign schedule to allow more child-led pacing)
   - Effect: Children less stressed, fewer meltdowns

### Mechanic Flow:
1. **Notice** — You get a thought bubble or journal entry: "Hmm, those plastic cups... I remember reading that Montessori recommends glass to teach careful handling."
2. **Wait for authority** — Grayed out "Fix" button with tooltip: "Need to be [tier] to change this"
3. **Fix** — At the right tier, you can spend resources (trust, time, maybe in-game money) to implement the change
4. **See results** — Children respond positively, trust meter increases, maybe unlock a cutscene where lead guide acknowledges your growth

### Progression:
- Fixing practices is OPTIONAL but rewarding
- Completionists can fix all 5 for a "Certified Montessori Environment" achievement
- Each fix improves classroom "quality" stat, which affects child happiness and learning speed

## Design Decisions (Answered 2026-02-07)

### 1. Difficulty Curve
**Decision:** Easy wins at the beginning (tutorial phase) to teach game mechanics, then open up so players can choose their challenge level. Cozy vibe where you can attempt harder or easier tasks as you prefer - no forced difficulty gating.

**Implementation:**
- First 2-3 days have guided "you can't fail" tutorial lessons
- After that, all unlocked lessons are available - you choose what to attempt
- Harder lessons (wrong timing, complex materials) = higher risk but bigger rewards
- Game teaches Montessori principles gradually, no prior knowledge required

---

### 2. Failure States
**Decision:** If a child isn't interested in a lesson you present, it wastes time. BUT if the lead guide is watching and you fail, it hurts your trust meter slightly.

**Implementation:**
- Failed lesson = time lost (could have been doing something else)
- If lead guide has "watching you" status active during failed lesson → -2 to -5 trust points
- Too many failures in a row → lead guide gives gentle feedback (teaching moment, not punishment)
- Game encourages observation before presentation (the Montessori way)

**Why this works:** Creates meaningful stakes without being punishing. Teaches players to observe children first, present second.

---

### 4. Child Personality Depth
**Decision:** 12 distinct children with personalities rooted in actual Montessori developmental theory (sensitive periods, temperaments, etc.). See detailed design in `child-personality-system.md`.

**Key features:**
- Each child has unique temperament, active sensitive periods, and developmental stage
- Personalities reflect real Montessori concepts (order-sensitive, language-focused, movement-driven, etc.)
- Observation mechanic reveals child's current state and needs
- Diverse representation (race, neurodiversity, family structures)
